It is a few days before the exam and you are holding a 300-page PDF. Rereading it from the first page will not fit in the time you have, and writing your own practice questions would take an entire afternoon. A faster option is a **PDF to quiz** tool: an AI reads your document and generates multiple-choice questions, and you spend your time answering them.

## Why a practice test beats rereading

Rereading feels productive because the text looks familiar, but familiarity is not recall. When you have to pick an answer, you force your brain to retrieve the idea, and that retrieval is what builds memory. A quiz also shows you right away which topics are weak, instead of letting you find out in the exam hall.

## 4 steps to make a quiz from a PDF with Rinkuzu

1. **Pick your PDF.** Use your own lecture slides, notes or a textbook chapter. A PDF with selectable text works best; blurry scans give weaker results. Only upload material you are allowed to use.
2. **Upload it.** Go to the [quiz creator](/en/create), choose the file and let Rinkuzu process it.
3. **Narrow the scope.** If the exam covers one chapter, use only that chapter. A short, focused quiz is easier to review than a huge one.
4. **Take the quiz, then review your mistakes.** Read every question you got wrong, then retake exactly those.

Not ready to sign up? You can [try it first](/en/try) and see how a quiz works.

## Tips for a better practice test

- **Split big documents.** One chapter or topic at a time beats a whole book.
- **Check the questions.** AI can make mistakes, so compare anything that looks odd with your textbook.
- **Repeat after a few days.** Spaced practice sticks better than cramming the night before.
- **Try personalized learning.** To go deeper, upload the same PDF to [personalized learning](/en/adaptive), which tracks which concepts you miss and gives you more of those.
